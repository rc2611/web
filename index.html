About Me

I am a PhD candidate in Water Resources Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Delhi (IIT Delhi), specializing in physical hydrology, monsoonal atmospheric processes, and hydro-climatological extremes. My research focuses on understanding the multi-scale atmospheric moisture transport dynamics that dictate extreme rainfall distribution and flood risk across South Asia using both data-driven diagnostic pipelines and process-based numerical models.

My academic foundation spans several premier engineering institutes in India. I completed my Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) in Civil Engineering at the National Institute of Technology Hamirpur (NIT Hamirpur). I subsequently earned my M.S. by Research in Water Resources Engineering at the Indian Institute of Technology Palakkad (IIT Palakkad), during which I was selected for a competitive semester exchange program at the Indian Institute of Technology Madras (IIT Madras) to undertake advanced computational fluid dynamics and hydrological modeling coursework.

At IIT Delhi, my doctoral research bridges atmospheric physics and surface hydrology to unravel the spatial and temporal predictability of severe hydrometeorological events under climate variability.

Research Details

My primary research investigates the atmospheric moisture transport pathways that drive extreme precipitation and catastrophic flooding across major Indian river basins. Central to this inquiry is a rigorous diagnostic evaluation of a comprehensive 40-year daily historical hydro-climatological dataset spanning 1981 through 2020.

Monsoonal Dynamics & Extreme Event Synchronization

Focusing on the June–July–August–September (JJAS) Indian Summer Monsoon season—with specialized emphasis on July weather dynamics when moisture convergence peaks—my work isolates high-threshold extreme precipitation events exceeding the 90th percentile. Using advanced non-linear event synchronization algorithms and lagged causality frameworks, I map the exact physical mechanisms through which atmospheric vapor fluxes transition into localized severe rainfall spells.

Global Climate Teleconnections & SST Reanalysis

To understand systemic drivers beyond continental boundaries, my research deciphers coupled ocean-atmosphere teleconnections linking Indian monsoon extremes to planetary climate modes. This includes quantifying phase dependencies and lagged correlations with the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO), El Niño-Southern Oscillation (ENSO), Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD), Southern Annular Mode (SAM), Arctic Oscillation (AO), and North Atlantic Oscillation (NAO).

Global Ocean Data Assimilation System (GODAS) Sea Surface Temperature (SST) reanalysis datasets are integrated into computational pipelines to track anomalous oceanic thermal signatures that precede atmospheric flux surges over South Asia.

Hydrological Modeling & SWAT Flux Accounting

Complementing data-driven analyses, I utilize the Soil and Water Assessment Tool (SWAT) to model catchment-scale water balances. My methodological contributions focus on process-interdependent calibration techniques that refine root zone soil moisture simulation. By explicitly accounting for soil moisture dynamics, my models improve the simulation accuracy of both surface runoff and subsurface hydrological fluxes during extreme monsoonal spells.
